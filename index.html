01 · The game

24 metres of fence. One rooftop garden. What would you build?

A mission starts in a child's own world. The student talks with a character, looks at the scene, and then does what builds memory: writes it down in a notebook. Nothing is typed into a chat box.

1 Meet the problem

Auntie Mei

Welcome to the rooftop garden! The estate gave us 24 metres of fence and a whole lot of seeds.

Auntie Mei

Every square metre grows one plant. How should I set the fence up so we grow the most?

Your mission

Grab your notebook. Sketch two different gardens that use all 24 metres, and work out how many plants each one grows.

✎ Pen and paper

2 Write it down

Garden plan
Garden A: 11 m × 1 m
fence = (11 + 1) × 2 = 24 m ✓
plants = 11 × 1 = 11
Garden B: 6 m × 6 m
fence = 6 × 4 = 24 m ✓
plants = 6 × 6 = 36
Same fence, more space: 36 plants!

02 · The catch

Thirty notebooks. One teacher. One lesson.

Writing is good for learning, but it hides what each student is thinking. Marking by hand takes about a day, and by then the lesson has moved on. Run both and watch the clock.

Time until the teacher knows 0:00

Illustrative simulation. The two timings are the real figures: about one day by hand, about five minutes with the dashboard.

04 · The reward

Understanding earns something you can keep.

When a student slips, the game offers a short challenge aimed at exactly that gap. Finish it and they collect coins, a Find card for the idea they just understood, and a place on their trophy shelf. Rewards are earned by what you did, never random.
